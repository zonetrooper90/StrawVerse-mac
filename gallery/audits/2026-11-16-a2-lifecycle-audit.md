## A2.1 — Player session ownership (high potential impact; runtime confirmation required)
- `src/js/11h-player-controls.js:29–59`: initializePlayer aborts previous controller, but only document keydown and fullscreenchange listeners use its signal. Media and text-track event listeners are not owned by it; loadedmetadata/error callbacks look up current player DOM; focusout schedules an untracked zero-delay timer. `11i-core-render.js:2,25,33–35` aborts controller outside playback or on next initialize, but replacement occurs before initialization on player-to-player render. `11b-player-page.js:50–56` stops demo, revokes object URL, clears controls timer, pauses video, resets Play Next on explicit Back. `11g-player-playback.js:163–175` advanceEpisode stops demo, revokes URL and clears timer, but does not explicitly pause/unload old media before replacing DOM. `11f-player-motion.js:46–72` play/pause morph is cancelled by sfResetPlayMorph on initialization, not explicitly on Back; finite frame loop may continue after exit. `11g-player-playback.js:32–43` demo requests frames even while paused (bounded to active simulation).
- `07a-watch-history.js:623–648`: playback session is global, history recording reads current state.playback and active installment rather than owning video identity; stale old media events could mutate another session or attribute progress to the wrong episode. Not a demonstrated corruption event. Node-local listeners alone do not prove a memory leak; media/queued callbacks and session identity are the risk.
- `11g-player-playback.js:139–160`: desktop openVideo and openInVLC awaits are not tied to initiating session; after await, current video is queried or captured video paused and current notice changed. `11h-player-controls.js:119–124` similar external-player notice. Delayed promise resolving after navigation or concurrent requests could affect new episode. Standalone bridge is absent normally; test with controlled stub, not actual native file picker. `11d-desktop-bridge.js:8–21` late initial preferences can trigger render; separate integration concern, do not redesign bridge or persistence without approval.
- Proposed A2.1 diagnostic first: instrument resource ownership and delayed bridge results in disposable browser contexts; synthetic short local media (no user file or artwork), demo playback, repeated Back/advance, stale metadata/error/timeupdate/seeked callbacks, delayed/reordered openVideo resolution. Assert no old video writes, no unexpected history entries, current session unchanged, no detached active media/RAF/global listener ownership; record before/after cleanup, use both builds and motion modes. Then discuss narrow unified player teardown with session token + listener abort, pause/unload, object URL revocation, controls/focus/morph cancellation; preserve approved player transition snapshots, focus, volume, subtitles, saved playback semantics and explicit local/VLC behavior. Do not change external playback semantics or claim native verification.
## A2.2 — Anime Details rail observer (medium; definite cleanup asymmetry)
- `11a-anime-detail.js:54–74` global activeInstallmentRailObserver; callback queries current screen rail. `11i-core-render.js:37–60` disconnects and replaces only within Details branch; history and primary-tab early returns (8–11) and Player branch (33–35) bypass it. `04j-tab-navigation.js:1–18` hides but retains screen on primary route. Old observer remains attached to rail and cards after leaving; may retain detached old targets after replacement, but at most one observer via current global owner, not established unbounded leak. Settings intentionally suspends retained screen; don't confuse hiding Settings with destruction.
- Proposed: disconnect/null at actual departure or before destruction, reconnect only on Details mount; leave deliberate Settings suspension and in-place installment selection intact. Diagnostic count observed targets and callback executions over repeated detail/primary/history/player routes; preserve rail scroll, arrow guards, snapping, Search origin restoration and reduced motion.
## A2.3 — History episode expansion listeners (medium; source-confirmed accumulation path)
- `07a-watch-history.js:459–488` whToggleRow adds new anonymous once transitionend listener each toggle. `src/styles/17-watch-history.css` disables transition in reduced motion; when no event fires, once listener is not removed. Repeated toggling same persistent DOM can accumulate callbacks until future event or destruction. Interrupted normal transitions may also leave listeners pending. No measured memory increase yet. whDestroyInner already destroys scrollers and progress observers at rerender/exit (455–503), and legacy preview controller explicitly removes listeners and observers (120–268).
- Proposed: one owned completion callback per row or motion-aware immediate update/cancellation; test repeated toggles (e.g. 100) normal/reduced plus reversals, motion change and route exit; record listener count and overlay refresh, preserve exact animation, expanded states, native scroll chain and keyboard semantics. Also measure History progress loop under Settings suspension: 356–414 uses visibility/intersection and explicit stop; don't assume hidden-page CPU leak without evidence.

## Healthy patterns / do not rewrite
- App-lifetime listeners in startup, persistent tab/workspace/route/modal roots and bootstrap register once; not duplicates merely because they remain throughout app lifetime.
- Tab-track animation cancels prior animation (`04i-tab-track-motion.js:18–19,47–56`); coordinated page fades cancel animations, clear busy state and apply queued transition (`04d-route-transitions.js:63–95`); preserve approved fades. Discover Search legacy restore owns one frame and checks identity/connection/surface with bounded completion (`19f-contextual-search.js:41–46,108–136`).
- History inner scrollers, progress observers/frames, recent-preview controller and color popup explicitly destroy/remove resources. No global lifecycle framework or mass listener rewrite warranted.
